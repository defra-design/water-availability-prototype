What are Hands-off Flow conditions?

'Hands-off Flow' (HoF) conditions come into effect when the flow rate drops below a certain level. When this happens most abstractors are issued an alert (although some abstractors self-monitor water levels) and abstraction is not allowed until the flow rate increases.

HoF conditions are measured in megalitres per day. One megalitre is equivalent to 1000 cubic metres. For reference, an Olympic sized swimming pool is 2.5 megalitres in size.

Your licence may have a different HoF condition applied depending on your use and exact abstraction point location.

What are Hands-off Flow bands?

Hands-off Flow (HoF) conditions are grouped into bands and this gives you an idea of how water stressed your local water body is.

Band 1 has the most water available and fewest restrictions, while band 6 is the most water stressed with no water available left for licensing.

Once the remaining water in the band has been licensed, the water body will move into the next band up and the restrictions will increase.

What is a common end date?

The 'common end date' indicates when the licensing strategy for this area is due for review. The Environment Agency review each local licensing strategy every 12 years.

Most licences expire on the common end date but sometimes a shorter time limit is needed, usually due to local issues or uncertainty. If you want to abstract water after the expiry date, you must apply for a new a licence to replace the existing one.

You can apply for a longer licence period of up to 24 years which is granted at the discretion of the Environment Agency.

If the next common end date is over 6 years away, it will usually apply to your licence as the expiry date. If it is less than 6 years away, in most cases we will skip to the next common end date and apply that to your licence instead.

Understanding groundwater availability

Water availability will depend on the location and depth of abstraction.

A pump test or hydrogeological impact assessment will be needed to determine licence availability for your specific abstraction point. You must contact the Environment Agency (see details below) to find out whether you need to get consent to investigate the groundwater source or prepare a hydrogeological impact assessment before you can apply for a licence.

Regulations and restrictions

Restrictions may apply to licences where availability is limited or to protect the environment.

Hands-off Flow and Hands-off Levels are set by the Environment Agency as conditions on licences, these prevent abstractors from taking water when levels drop below a certain point.
